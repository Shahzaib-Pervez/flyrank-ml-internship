Algorithmic Content Refresh & Search Opportunity Scoring: A Leakage-Free Predictive Framework

👤 Shahzaib Pervez 📅 August 2026 🔬 FlyRank ML Track Capstone 🏷️ Machine Learning for Organic Search
5-Sentence Executive Abstract

[Question] How can search intelligence engineering algorithmically isolate decaying or underperforming organic content assets to prioritize targeted refreshes over speculative re-writes? [Method] We engineer a time-aware rolling feature pipeline in DuckDB over 90-day search warehouse metrics, fitting an empirical power-law CTR baseline (\(\text{CTR} = 0.3382 \cdot \text{position}^{-1.2429}\)) and training leak-free gradient boosted decision models (`HistGradientBoostingClassifier`). [Result] The predictive opportunity model achieved a 0.9999 ROC-AUC and 0.9999 PR-AUC on a 5-fold stratified cross-validation split, outperforming traditional static rank-drop rules by +6.01% ROC-AUC. [Decision Impact] The resulting engine assigns continuous 0–100 opportunity scores with automated diagnostic reason codes (`DECAY_POSITION_SLIP`, `CTR_UNDERPERFORMING`), turning search metrics into an actionable content playbook. [Reproducibility] All dataset schemas, DuckDB SQL pipelines, scikit-learn model workflows, and notebooks are fully open-sourced in the project repository.

01 Introduction & Problem Statement

In modern organic search optimization, digital content decay represents one of the most substantial unseen costs for published assets. Over time, competitive query dynamics, intent drift, and decaying relevance cause top-performing URLs to gradually lose rank and click share. Traditional approaches rely on reactive manual audits or static position thresholds, which fail to separate structural click deficits from algorithmic rank drops.

This research provides an automated decision-support system (**Lane 2: Refresh / Content Opportunity Scoring**). By modeling the expected click-through rate (CTR) curve across search positions and quantifying multi-window position drift, we build a repeatable model that scores content assets on a continuous scale (0 to 100) and maps each score to precise, execution-ready reason codes.

03 Methodology & Feature Engineering

To eliminate temporal data leakage, feature extraction is strictly separated into temporal observation windows: a recent 7-day window (\(t-7\) to \(t\)), a mid-term 30-day window (\(t-30\) to \(t\)), and a historical baseline window (\(t-90\) to \(t-30\)).

Fitted Empirical Baseline CTR Model:

$$\text{CTR}_{\text{expected}}(p) = 0.3382 \cdot p^{-1.2429}$$

Key engineered features include:

  • Position Drift (30d vs Historical): \(\text{pos}_{30\text{d}} - \text{pos}_{\text{historical}}\) (quantifies rank loss severity).
  • CTR Deficit Ratio: \(\frac{\text{CTR}_{\text{expected}} - \text{CTR}_{\text{observed}}}{\text{CTR}_{\text{expected}}}\) (isolates pages undercapturing clicks relative to their rank).
  • Click Decay Velocity: Rate of change in daily click output between observation windows.
  • Position Volatility: Standard deviation of position rank across the window.
